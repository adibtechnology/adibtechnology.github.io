How Does Light Reflection Work?

Have you ever wondered how we can see an object in a mirror even when it is not directly in front of us? This simple observation reveals an interesting property of light: reflection.

1. Introduction

We can see an object when light from that object reaches our eyes. Most objects reflect some of the light falling on them.

Light travels at approximately 300,000 kilometres per second in a vacuum. Because it travels so fast, ordinary cameras cannot easily show its journey in slow motion.

2. A Real-Life Observation: The Mirror and the Drum

Imagine that there is a large mirror in your room. A drum is placed beside the mirror, while you stand somewhere from which you cannot see the drum directly.

However, when you look at the mirror, you can see the drum's image!

Research Question:

If the drum is not directly in front of the mirror, how can its image reach our eyes through the mirror?

3. Scientific Explanation

This happens because of the reflection of light. The process can be explained in a few steps:

  • Light falls on the surface of the drum.
  • The drum reflects light in different directions.
  • Some of that reflected light reaches the mirror.
  • The mirror reflects the light towards our eyes.
  • Our eyes receive the light, allowing us to see the drum's image.

4. Understanding the Law of Reflection

When light strikes a smooth mirror, it reflects according to a scientific rule called the Law of Reflection.

Angle of Incidence = Angle of Reflection

The angle at which light strikes the mirror is equal to the angle at which it reflects.

The direction of reflected light depends on the direction of incoming light and the orientation of the reflecting surface.

5. Experiment and Observation

A torch and a mirror can help us observe how reflected light changes direction.

When light from the torch strikes the mirror, it reflects in a direction determined by the angle of incidence and the mirror's position.

6. Conclusion

This observation helps us understand that an object does not always need to be directly in front of our eyes for us to see it.

Light reflected from an object can reach a mirror and then travel towards our eyes. This is how mirrors allow us to see objects that are not directly visible.
